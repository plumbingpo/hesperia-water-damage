Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch

The Benefits of Professional Flood Cleanup

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Elevate appliances and electrical systems in flood-prone parts of your property
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
